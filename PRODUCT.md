# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Primarily Elixir developers evaluating frameworks, comparing Francis against Phoenix and raw Plug. They know Elixir; they're deciding in under two minutes whether Francis is worth their time. Secondarily, developers who are newer to Elixir and need the ecosystem to feel accessible and welcoming, not gatekept.

## Product Purpose

Marketing landing page for Francis, a lightweight DSL for Elixir web apps built on Plug and Bandit. The site exists to convert curious developers into people who have typed `mix francis.new my_app`. Success looks like a developer understanding what Francis is, seeing that it's approachable, and immediately having the commands they need to start.

## Brand Personality

Simple, honest, friendly. No overselling. The page earns trust by showing real code and getting out of the way. Warmth comes from clarity, not decoration.

## Anti-references

- Phoenix/LiveView site: no purple gradients, no modern SaaS landing page patterns.
- Generic SaaS starters: no hero + 3-feature grid + CTA button template.
- Heavy documentation sites: this is a landing, not a Hexdocs clone.
- Hacker aesthetic: no terminal green on black, no ASCII art borders.

## Design Principles

1. **Practice what you preach.** Francis reduces boilerplate. The site should too. No sections that exist because landing pages usually have them.
2. **Show, don't tell.** Code examples are the argument. Don't describe what Francis does when you can demonstrate it in four lines.
3. **Approachable from step one.** Both an experienced Elixir dev and someone new to the ecosystem should immediately understand the value and feel capable of trying it.
4. **Earn trust through honesty.** No marketing fluff. State what it is, show how it works, link to real docs. If it's a limitation, don't hide it.
5. **Simplicity is the feature.** The aesthetic mirrors the framework's value proposition: remove noise, keep only what matters.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AAA. Strong contrast ratios throughout, full keyboard navigation, screen reader semantics on all interactive elements. No motion that can't be suppressed via `prefers-reduced-motion`.
